    The drivers and the items

    The items. Drive through a box to get one. Collective effervescence (Durkheim) gives you a burst of speed. The iron cage (Weber, as translated by Parsons) is dropped behind you and traps whoever hits it. Anomie (Durkheim) leaves everyone ahead of you without norms, so they wobble and slow down. Revolution (Marx) overthrows whoever is in first place. The stranger (Simmel), near and far at once, lets you pass straight through traps and rivals for a few seconds. Durkheim, Weber and Marx are twice as likely to draw their own concept.

    The drivers. Hannah Arendt, The Origins of Totalitarianism (1951). Simone de Beauvoir, The Second Sex (1949). W. E. B. Du Bois, The Souls of Black Folk (1903). Émile Durkheim, Suicide (1897). Karl Marx, Capital (1867). Max Weber, The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism (1905). Ida B. Wells, Southern Horrors (1892).

    Watch out for Reviewer 2. The puddles on the track spin you out.
